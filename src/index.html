Tiger conservation in India

Save the Tigers.
Protect India's apex predator.

India is home to the majority of the world's wild tigers. The numbers have been climbing for fifteen years, but the recovery is fragile and uneven. Here is what the official data says, what still threatens them, and how you can help.

03 — The challenges

What tigers still face

A rising national total doesn't mean every landscape is secure. In the 2018 and 2022 estimates, Mizoram and Nagaland each recorded zero tigers.

Habitat loss & fragmentation

Roads, mining, farming and growing settlements cut the corridors tigers need to disperse, find mates and reach new territory. Isolated populations are far more vulnerable.

What helps: protecting wildlife corridors and keeping reserves connected.

Poaching & illegal trade

Organised wildlife crime targets tiger skins and body parts. Across all species, India registered 2,701 wildlife-crime cases between 2020 and 2024.

What helps: intelligence sharing, trained front-line staff, and public reporting of suspicious activity.

Human–tiger conflict

As tiger numbers rise and people live closer to forests, livestock losses and retaliation become more likely, hurting both families and tigers.

What helps: quick compensation, early-warning systems and community-led programmes.

Report

Report poaching or illegal trade

A 2022 environment ministry notice says the national toll-free number 1800 11 9334 can be used to report illegal wildlife trade and poaching. You can also contact your local forest department. Please confirm current numbers on official sites.

Wildlife Crime Control Bureau →
Travel

Visit tigers responsibly

  • Choose licensed operators and follow forest department rules.
  • Keep quiet, keep your distance, never chase or crowd an animal.
  • Don't feed wildlife or leave litter.
  • Don't post live locations of sightings.
